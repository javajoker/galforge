# The Umbrella We Shared

*(original short fiction — pipeline input)*

The rain came down like it had a grudge.

I stood under the dripping eave of the bus stop, school bag held over my head like that
would help, and watched the last bus of the afternoon pull away without me. Of course it
did. The timetable and I had never been on speaking terms.

"You missed it too, huh?"

A girl ducked under the shelter beside me, shaking water off a bright yellow umbrella. Nao
Hidaka — same class as me, the kind of person who treated every disaster like a punchline.
She grinned at the empty road. "Twins. We're disaster twins now."

"I prefer 'unlucky acquaintances,'" I said.

"Boring." She nudged my arm. Thunder cracked somewhere over the hills, and for half a second
her grin slipped into something startled before snapping right back.

The shelter's bench creaked. I hadn't noticed her at first — Shizuku Aono, sitting very still
at the far end with a book closed on her lap, watching the rain like it was telling her a
secret. She was in our year too, though I'd never heard her say more than a sentence at a
time.

"...The next bus isn't for forty minutes," Shizuku said quietly, not looking up. "If you're
counting on it."

"See, this one's got the timetable memorized," Nao announced, dropping onto the bench. "Hey,
Shizuku, you want to share mine when it clears? Yellow's lucky."

Before Shizuku could answer, a third voice barreled in from the rain — Rui, Nao's friend from
the track team, jogging up with a newspaper plastered uselessly to her head.

"Nao! There you are! Coach is going to *kill* us, the meet got moved up—" Rui skidded under
the shelter, saw me and Shizuku, and blinked. "Oh. Sorry. Am I interrupting a thing?"

"There's no thing," Shizuku said, at the exact moment Nao said, "Total thing."

Rui looked between us, delighted, then grabbed Nao's wrist. "Tell me later. We have to *go*,
the bus on Maple still runs—" And just like that she dragged Nao back out into the downpour,
yellow umbrella popping open over both of them.

"Catch you tomorrow, disaster twin!" Nao called over her shoulder. Then they were gone, and
it was just me, Shizuku, the rain, and forty minutes.

Something glinted on the bench where Nao had been sitting. A little charm, shaped like a tiny
brass bus, its ring snapped open — fallen off her bag, probably. I picked it up. It was warm.

Shizuku had finally looked up from the rain. "She's always losing things," she said. A pause.
"...Do you want to wait? It's a long forty minutes alone." Another pause, and the smallest
breath of a smile. "I have an umbrella too. It's not lucky. But it's dry."

I looked at the brass bus in my palm, and at the road where the yellow umbrella had vanished,
and at the girl on the bench who had said more to me in one minute than in a whole year.

Forty minutes. A charm to return. A quiet voice, or a loud one.

I had to decide which rain to walk into.
